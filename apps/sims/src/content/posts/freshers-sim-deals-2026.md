Sign a 12 month SIM contract in Freshers' Week without checking its price rise date, and the student discount that looked cheapest in September can cost more than an ordinary rolling plan by next spring.

The cheapest SIM for a fresher is whichever card above, the student pick or the plain cheapest pick, still costs less once you add its stated mid-contract price rise. It also needs signal at your halls and around campus. Twelve of the twenty five UK networks we track state a real student offer on their own pages. The rest state none we could confirm. Every figure below comes from a network's own page or terms, dated, and the cards above are rechecked every Monday.

## What is the cheapest SIM deal for freshers?

It's whichever of the two cards above still costs less after its price rise, not automatically the one marked for students. Open both and compare directly. iD Mobile's own student SIM page states unlimited 5G data, minutes and texts for £15 a month. Compare it against the cheapest card rather than assuming the word student means cheaper.

giffgaff's promotions terms state 100GB a month for 12 months on its £10 monthly rolling plan, for members who verify with a .ac.uk email address, between 4 August 2026 and 4 August 2027. After the twelve months, the terms say you may be moved to a plan of equivalent price, so check what that costs nearer the date.

If neither card suits you, [our students page](/students) lists every offer we could verify, and [our full comparison of student SIM prices](/blog/cheapest-sim-only-deal-for-students/) goes through each one.

## Here is exactly what to do

1. Check signal for the host network, not just the brand printed on the SIM. Simp and iD Mobile run on Three's masts, giffgaff runs on O2's, Lycamobile runs on EE's, and VOXI and Talkmobile run on Vodafone's. [Our data guide](/blog/best-uk-network-for-data/) shows how to check a postcode.
2. Decide between a 30 day rolling plan and a fixed 12 or 24 month contract, based on how sure you are that you are staying at that address for the whole year.
3. Open both cards above and compare the monthly price, the total cost and the price rise shown on each, not just the label.
4. If a network states a student offer, claim it the way its own page describes. Simp, EE and Lycamobile verify through Student Beans, Three and giffgaff check a .ac.uk email address directly, and Vodafone uses its own online form, submitted within 60 days of connecting.
5. If you take a 12 or 24 month contract, note the month its price rises in your calendar, from the table below, so a bigger bill in spring is not a surprise.
6. Keep your old number if you are switching rather than joining fresh. Ofcom's switching page says you text PAC to 65075, your current provider must reply within one minute, and the code is valid for 30 days. [Our switching guide](/blog/how-to-switch-mobile-network-and-keep-your-number/) has the rest.

## Should I get a 30 day rolling SIM or a 12 month contract for my first term?

A rolling plan suits a first term if you are not certain you are staying at the same address for the year. It also removes one worry if you have no UK credit history yet. giffgaff's help pages state no credit checks are carried out when you buy a plan, and that it only checks credit if you take a phone on a loan. SMARTY's own page, titled No Credit Check SIM Only Deals, states its SIM only deals are paid up front on a rolling monthly plan and do not require a credit check. Lycamobile's Student Beans page states its student plans have no contract and no credit checks.

We found nothing stated either way for Vodafone, Three, EE or Simp SIM only plans. O2's eligibility checker page states its first check will not affect your credit rating, but a full credit assessment happens later in checkout.

A rolling plan can cost you the discount, though. Vodafone's student terms state the 10% offer applies to a 12 month SIM only plan and exclude the 30 day SIM only plan altogether. That means a Vodafone fresher cannot have both the flexibility and the discount. giffgaff's own plans roll month to month even though its no price rise promise is written for an 18 month contract.

## Will my price go up before the year is out?

Only on some networks: seven of the eleven below charge nothing extra, the other four state a fixed monthly rise. Since January 2025, Ofcom has required any price rise written into a contract to be shown in pounds and pence at the point of sale, not as a percentage or an inflation figure. That rule is why every row below shows a cash amount or the words no price rise, never a percentage alone.

Vodafone's 10% student discount only applies to its 12 month SIM only plan, and that plan will meet Vodafone's own price rise of £2.50 a month each 1 April. Sign up this September and you cross that date about seven months in. EE is listed here for its published policy only, never as an affiliate deal on this site. It adds £2.50 a month each 31 March to pay monthly and SIM only plans taken under its current terms. A 24 month EE contract can therefore cross that date more than once.

Three's rise depends on the plan's data allowance: £1.80 a month on plans of 4GB or less, £1.90 a month from 5GB to 99GB, and £2.30 a month on 100GB or more. It lands each 1 April, for anyone joining or upgrading from 9 November 2025. O2's airtime plans rise by £2.50 a month each April, and its data only and smartwatch plans by 75p.

SMARTY, giffgaff, Lebara, Talkmobile, Simp and Ecotalk state no mid-contract price rise at all. iD Mobile states none on its SIM only range either, though its phone plans carry a rise it does not put a figure to.

## What happens to my data if I go home for the holidays or study abroad?

None of the roaming policies we checked are built for a full year away. They are fair use additions to a UK plan, meant for periodic trips, not for living abroad on a year in industry or an exchange.

O2's international page states inclusive Europe Zone roaming is for periodic travel only, and using it for more than 63 days in any four month period triggers surcharges. giffgaff's roaming help page states inclusive roaming needs recent UK usage and lasts up to 63 days per trip. If a placement or exchange runs longer than that, plan on a local SIM once you are there.

For an ordinary holiday, the caps above are what you get. Among the deals we actually list, they run from 5GB on giffgaff and Talkmobile up to 30GB on Lebara. EE's cap is higher, at 50GB, but EE is editorial only, not a deal here. iD Mobile currently offers the full UK allowance while its cap stays suspended, and Ecotalk charges £2 a day instead with no EU roaming included.
