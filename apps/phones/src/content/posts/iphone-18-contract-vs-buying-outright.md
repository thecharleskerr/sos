## Which is cheaper, an iPhone 18 contract or buying outright?

Whichever route has the lower total over the same number of months. Neither wins by default. Two sums decide it.

Sum one is the contract: the monthly price times the number of months, plus the upfront cost, plus every stated price rise for the months it applies. The card above prints the monthly price, the upfront cost, the total contract cost and the rise in pounds and pence.

Sum two is outright: £1,299 for the 256GB iPhone 18 Pro, whether paid in full or as Apple's 30 payments of £43.30. Add a SIM only plan for the same number of months, plus that plan's own stated rise. Take the SIM only plan from the live picks on our sister site, [Save on Sims](https://saveonsims.co.uk/). Each card there prints the same lines and is re-checked every Monday. If your plan carries a rise, the sister site's [price rise calculator](https://saveonsims.co.uk/price-rise-calculator/) adds it up for the months you have left.

The lower total wins, provided its allowance covers what you use. With a phone at £1,299, sum two on a no-rise SIM is a fixed number that does not move for two years, which is why it is the one every contract has to beat.

Two rules keep the sum honest. Ofcom's inflation ban page says that from 17 January 2025 any rise in a new contract must be set out in pounds and pence at the point of sale. The CMA's price transparency guidance on gov.uk says mandatory charges belong in the headline price and hiding fees until later is illegal.

## What is split pricing and why does it change the sum?

Split pricing is when a network bills the phone and the airtime as two separate agreements, and it matters because only one half rises. O2's help page says O2 Refresh splits your bill into a Device Plan for the phone and an Airtime Plan for data, texts and minutes. The Device Plan is a set amount each month over 24 months. O2's device plan page calls it a loan agreement with no interest. EE's Flex Pay help page says Flex Pay separates the cost of the handset from the airtime plan as an interest free consumer credit agreement over 12, 24, 36 or 48 months. EE is described here for its published policy only and is never sold as a deal on this site.

The rise lands on the airtime half. O2's annual price increase page says each April the airtime plan goes up by £2.50 a month on voice plans, and the cost of your device stays frozen. EE's plan terms in force from 7 August 2025, checked 4 September 2026, put its rise at £2.50 a month on 31 March for plans not sold at a fixed price. Whether that rise reaches the airtime half of a Flex Pay contract is not stated in EE's terms, so check the figure shown on your own agreement.

One catch follows. O2's device plan page says cancelling the airtime plan in the first 24 months means paying the rest of the device plan in full. EE's Flex Pay page says the same inside its 24 month minimum term. Once the phone is paid off, O2 says you pay only the airtime plan and EE says the device contract ceases on its own. Ofcom requires your provider to tell you 10 to 40 days before the airtime contract ends. Our guide on [getting out of a phone contract early](/blog/how-to-get-out-of-a-phone-contract-early/) covers the exit costs.

## What if I do not have the cash upfront?

Then compare the two credit routes, because paying in full is not the only way to run sum two. Apple's UK shop page gives its own figure for the 256GB iPhone 18 Pro: 30 payments of £43.30 at 0% APR, which is £1,299, checked 9 September 2026. Apple's separate financing page describes a Flexible Finance Account from Creation, a line of credit with 0% APR across iPhone models, and a one-off application that covers future purchases too. Add a SIM only card and you have sum two without a lump sum.

A network split contract is credit too. O2's device plan page says you make an upfront payment on any product on a device plan. So the question is the same on both sides: what does each ask on day one, and what is the total by the end. Networks that state no rise on the airtime keep that total still: SMARTY and giffgaff both say so on their own pages, checked 2 September 2026.

## What if I want the newest iPhone every year?

Then the trade-in value decides it. Apple's UK upgrade page says the iPhone Upgrade Programme is ending and points to the Flexible Finance Account instead. Depending on the offer you choose you can upgrade every year or two, and a trade-in in good condition pays off the remaining loan balance. Apple has not stated a trade-in range for the iPhone 18 Pro itself, checked 9 September 2026.

A network contract runs 24 months on the airtime side, and O2's device plan page says you can pay the device plan off at any time. Upgrading early means clearing that balance first, fine if the trade-in covers it and expensive if it does not. Get the trade-in quote in writing before you sign.

## Will I pass a credit check?

Every route that spreads the phone's cost is a loan, so every one has a check. O2's device plan page says the device plan needs a credit check because it is a loan agreement, subject to status. EE's Flex Pay page says credit for Flex Pay is subject to a credit check, your status and assessment. Apple's financing page describes a one-off application to Creation.

O2 offers a way to find out first. Its eligibility checker page says the checker tells you whether you are likely to pass, and using it does not affect your credit rating. Paying in full has no check at all. Our [credit check guide](/blog/phone-contract-credit-check/) covers what happens if you are turned down, and how SIM only credit checks vary by network.
