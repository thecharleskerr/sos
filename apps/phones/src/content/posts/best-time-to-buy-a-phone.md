Buy a phone in its first few weeks on sale and you pay the highest price it will ever carry. That is the cost of getting the timing wrong. The best time to buy a phone is later in its cycle, once the maker's own price has settled, or once a contract's full total beats buying outright. The same phone then costs less without being any worse. Our figures come from the networks' and makers' own pages, dated below. The deal cards on this site are rechecked every Monday, so working this out for yourself takes about ten minutes.

## When does a phone's price actually move?

A phone is dearest on the day it goes on sale. That is the only price the maker has set for it. From there, two things move what you actually pay. A successor arrives, and that pushes the outgoing model's price down. A network changes its own offers and its mid-contract price rise, and that moves the total month to month, regardless of the phone's age.

Apple's newsroom dates the iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 17 Pro Max announcement to September 2025. The Pro starts at £1,099 and the Pro Max at £1,199, both stated on Apple's own page. The standard iPhone 17 starts at £799 for 256GB, on Apple's UK store. Apple introduced the iPhone 17e in March 2026, per its newsroom page, at £599 for 256GB of storage. That is twice the storage of the phone it replaced, at the same starting price. That is the launch cycle in miniature: a new phone lands at a fixed price, and whatever it replaces immediately looks like worse value at the same money.

Samsung's UK store listed the Galaxy S26 from £879 for 256GB, the S26 Plus from £1,099 and the S26 Ultra from £1,279, checked against Samsung's own pages on 2 September 2026. None of Samsung's, Apple's or Google's pages we checked state a schedule of price cuts after launch, so we cannot tell you a phone will be a fixed amount cheaper by a fixed date. What we can tell you is that the RRP is set once, at launch. Everything after that is either a network's contract pricing, or the maker's own store price on the day you look. That is why the check below matters more than guessing a month.

## Here is exactly what to do

1. Find the maker's current UK price for the exact phone and storage you want, on their own site, and note the date you checked it. Prices above are examples, not this week's offer.
2. Look at the total contract cost on this week's pick above. Add it up over the full length of the contract, not just the monthly figure.
3. Add up what buying outright plus a separate SIM only plan would cost you over the same number of months. [Save on Sims](https://saveonsims.co.uk/) lists current SIM only prices if you want to price that half yourself.
4. Compare the two totals. If the contract does not beat buying outright plus a SIM only plan, price up buying outright and adding a SIM later. Picking last year's model instead is worth pricing up too.
5. Check which network is behind the deal, and whether it publishes a mid-contract price rise, using the table below. A rise in pounds and pence changes the real total, even when the monthly price looks fixed.
6. Recheck around Black Friday, 27 November 2026, and again whenever this site refreshes its deal cards each Monday. Neither date guarantees the lowest price on its own.

## What networks actually charge extra during your contract

Since January 2025, Ofcom's rules require any mid-contract rise to be shown in pounds and pence, rather than as a vague inflation link. That is why the figures below exist to be checked at all.

| Network | Mid-contract price rise | When it lands |
|---|---|---|
| O2 | £2.50 a month on voice plans, 75p on data-only and smartwatch plans; the device part is frozen | Each April |
| Vodafone | £2.50 a month on pay monthly airtime and data plans; Device Plans are not affected | Each 1 April |
| Three | £1.80 to £2.30 a month depending on the plan's data allowance, for plans taken on or after 9 November 2025; device payments are excluded | Each 1 April |
| EE | £2.50 a month on pay monthly and SIM only plans taken from 7 August 2025, unless sold as a fixed price plan | Each 31 March |
| SMARTY | No price rise | Not applicable |
| giffgaff | No price rise, on 18 month contracts | Not applicable |

EE appears here for information only. Save on Smartphones does not list EE as an affiliate deal, so nothing above compares its pricing against another network's card.

## Should I just wait for Black Friday?

Black Friday, 27 November 2026, is a real date and worth checking. It is not the only one that matters, though, so it is not automatically the best time to buy a phone for everyone. This site rechecks every deal card each Monday, all year, so a total worth taking can appear well outside that one week. Treat 27 November as one date to look on, not the date. Keep running the sum in the steps above whenever you are actually ready to buy.

## Won't last year's model already feel out of date?

The hardware does not change after launch. Software support continues on whatever schedule the maker states. Where a maker publishes a fixed commitment, we have found it. Google's own store page for the Pixel 10a states seven years of OS and security updates. Samsung's newsroom states the Galaxy S26 series carries seven generations of OS upgrades and seven years of security updates from its initial global launch date. Samsung's page for the Galaxy A17 5G states six generations of OS upgrades and six years of security updates from its first global launch date. Not every model we checked carries a stated figure. Two dedicated searches of Apple's own pages for the iPhone 17e found no fixed number of years of software updates, so that field is not stated. Buying an older model does not change that either way. Check the specific phone's own update commitment before assuming an older model is worse off.

## Doesn't the price just rise again once you're on a contract?

Possibly, but only the airtime half, not the handset. The table above is built from each network's own pricing page, and it shows real variation. O2, Vodafone and Three all apply a fixed rise once a year. EE does the same for the plans in force from 7 August 2025. SMARTY and giffgaff state plainly that they do not. Three's own rise is banded by data allowance: £1.80 a month up to 4GB, £1.90 from 5GB to 99GB, and £2.30 for 100GB or more. That is exactly why step 5 above tells you to check the network behind a deal, before comparing its total to buying outright. A phone that looks cheaper on the card can still end up costing more over the full term, if its network's rise is bigger than a rival's. The total contract cost, not the first month's price, is what you should be comparing.
