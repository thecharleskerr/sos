Order the wrong SIM and you wait days for a card to arrive before you can even try the plan, which turns switching into a chore you keep putting off. An eSIM changes that. It downloads onto your phone in minutes, so trying a different network becomes something you do on a Tuesday evening, not something you plan a week around. Most UK networks now offer it, though not on every plan, and the limits are worth knowing before you buy.

Short answer: yes, on some plans. O2, Vodafone, Three, EE, SMARTY, giffgaff, iD Mobile, VOXI, Lebara, Tesco Mobile and Sky Mobile all offer eSIM on at least some SIM only or pay monthly plans, and Simp sells no physical SIM at all, only eSIM. Every claim below comes from each network's own pages, checked on 2 September 2026, and this week's short-contract pick above is re-checked every Monday.

## Why does eSIM make it easier to try a rolling SIM only plan?

The point of an eSIM is speed. SMARTY says you can join instantly, without waiting for a new SIM to arrive in the post. VOXI says you can be online in as little as 10 minutes. giffgaff says you can be up and running in minutes, and iD Mobile says you can buy a plan and get connected within minutes. None of that is possible with a physical SIM, which still has to travel through the post before you can use it.

That speed matters most on a rolling monthly SIM only plan, the kind you can leave whenever you like. If a plan turns out to be wrong for you, an eSIM lets you download a replacement the same day rather than wait for the next card to arrive. ## What happens to my eSIM if my phone is lost, stolen or broken?

You cannot pop an eSIM out and put it in another phone the way you can with a physical SIM, because it is built into the handset. O2 says you can move your eSIM to another compatible device whenever you like, and your number and plan transfer across, but that only works if the original phone still switches on. If it is broken, lost or stolen, you need another way back online.

Check how your network handles this before you buy. giffgaff's help pages say its eSIMs are activated through the giffgaff app only, so if your handset will not switch on, you need a second device or a shop visit to get going again. Vodafone, EE, SMARTY, Three and others say you can order a replacement through the app, your online account or in store instead, which is worth knowing before your only working phone dies on you.

## Can I use an eSIM alongside a physical SIM?

Yes, on most modern phones. SMARTY's help pages say many phones support dual SIM, meaning you can have one physical SIM and one or more eSIM profiles active on the same device at once. Apple's own support pages describe the same dual SIM setup on iPhone.

This suits anyone who wants a personal number on their usual SIM and a separate number, for work or for a short-term deal, running on an eSIM without carrying a second phone. Check your specific handset supports it before you rely on it, since not every device does.

## Should I buy a travel eSIM or use my network's roaming abroad?

That depends what your own network already includes. Some UK networks bundle EU roaming into the plan with no daily charge, and a travel eSIM would then just add an extra cost for something you already have. Others charge by the day or do not include roaming at all, and a separate travel eSIM data plan can work out cheaper for a short trip.

## How does switching to an eSIM actually work?

The exact steps differ slightly by network, but the shape is the same on iPhone and Android. Apple's own support pages describe three ways in: eSIM Carrier Activation, where you scan a QR code with the Camera app and tap the Cellular Plan Detected notification, eSIM Quick Transfer, where a new iPhone pulls the eSIM across from your previous one without contacting the network, and converting an existing physical SIM to an eSIM from within Settings.

On Android, UK networks describe a similar pattern in their own words. Three says you scan the QR code emailed to you, then enter a confirmation code sent in a second email. SMARTY and Tesco Mobile describe the same two-step pattern: scan the code, then enter a code to confirm. You need a wifi or mobile data connection throughout, and most networks say activation finishes within minutes, though some warn it can take longer at busy times.

## Here is exactly what to do

1. **Check your phone supports eSIM.** Dial *#06# and look for an EID number in the pop up, per Vodafone's and iD Mobile's own instructions.
2. **Choose a SIM only plan and pick eSIM.** Select it instead of a physical SIM at checkout. Most networks offer both at no price difference.
3. **Wait for the email.** It usually contains a QR code, and on some networks a separate confirmation code.
4. **Connect to wifi, then scan the QR code.** Use your phone's camera or settings, and follow the on-screen prompts.
5. **Enter the confirmation code if asked**, then set the new eSIM as your default for calls and data.
6. **Keep your old SIM working** until the new eSIM is confirmed live, then cancel or recycle it.

eSIM will not change your monthly price or your allowance. What it changes is the time between deciding to switch and actually being connected, which on most networks is now minutes rather than days.
