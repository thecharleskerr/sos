The cheapest iPhone 18 deal is the one that costs you the least by the day the contract ends, and choosing by the monthly figure alone is how people pay more for the same phone. On 2 September 2026 Apple had not announced the iPhone 18 or published a UK price, so there is no deal to name yet. This guide gives you the method instead. Every figure in it comes from the seller's own pages and carries its checked date, so on launch day you can run the sums in about ten minutes.

## Is the iPhone 18 on sale in the UK yet?

No. We checked apple.com/uk on 2 September 2026. The newest model on Apple's UK buy page was the iPhone 17, listed from £799 or £26.63 a month at 0% APR. There was no iPhone 18 page and no UK price. Apple's newsroom carried no iPhone 18 announcement on the same date.

## What makes one iPhone 18 deal cheaper than another?

The total you pay over the term, not the monthly figure. The sum is simple: monthly price times the number of months, plus the upfront cost, plus every stated price rise for the months it applies.

A low monthly price with a large upfront payment can cost more than a higher monthly price with nothing upfront. A 36 month device plan lowers the monthly figure and adds a year of payments. The card on this site prints the total so you never do the multiplication in your head.

The price rise matters because it is now a fixed number. Ofcom's page on the ban says that from 17 January 2025 providers are prohibited from including inflation-linked or percentage-based price rise terms in new contracts, and that any rise must be set out in pounds and pence at the point of sale. If a seller cannot tell you the rise in pounds, walk away.

## Here is exactly what to do

1. Open the [homepage](/) on or after launch day and note the total cost on each iPhone 18 card. Ignore the monthly price for now. The total is the number you compare.
2. Read the price rise line on the same card. It says the rise in pounds and pence, or No price rise. Count the months after the rise lands, multiply, and add that to the total. Do it for every rise date inside the term.
3. Price the phone on its own. Apple's UK buy page lists the SIM free price and the 0% APR monthly option for each storage size. Note the size the network deal actually gives you, because the two must match.
4. Add a SIM only plan to Apple's price for the same number of months. Pick one on our sister site, [Save on Sims](https://saveonsims.co.uk/), where every card prints the same total and price rise line. Phone plus SIM only is the number the network deal has to beat.
5. Subtract trade-in on both sides. Get an estimate for your old phone from Apple's Trade In page and from the network. Use the same old phone value in both sums so the comparison is fair.
6. Take the lower total, then check the data, minutes, texts and roaming on the card cover what you use. A cheap total with an allowance you will exceed is not cheap.

## Should I wait for the iPhone 18 price to drop?

Nobody official publishes a schedule of iPhone price cuts, so we cannot tell you when or whether the iPhone 18 will get cheaper. What Apple's own pages show is the alternative to waiting. On 2 September 2026 apple.com/uk still listed the iPhone 16 alongside the iPhone 17, so last year's model stays on sale when the new one arrives. Apple's refurbished store lists iPhones at up to 15% off, with a one year warranty, a new battery and a new outer shell. Our guide on [whether to buy a refurbished iPhone](/blog/should-you-buy-a-refurbished-iphone/) covers when that beats new.

Waiting does not move the price rise either. It lands on the network's fixed date whatever month you sign. Three's terms for customers joining on or after 9 November 2025 put it on 1 April. EE's put it on 31 March during the minimum term. O2 and Vodafone state each April. Signing in March does not dodge the April rise.

## Is trade-in worth it?

Yes, when you treat it as a cut to the upfront cost and put it into the total on both sides. Apple's iPhone 17 page offered £30 to £680 off when you trade in an iPhone X or newer, checked 2 September 2026, so the gap between a worn old phone and a recent one is wide. Apple's Trade In page says the credit lowers the amount you finance if you pay monthly, that the old phone must be sent within 14 days of receiving the new one, and that Apple contacts you before changing the value if the inspected condition differs from what you described.

Networks run their own trade-in schemes. Their values are not on pages we can quote, so get a written quote before you rely on one. Our guide to [what your old phone is worth](/blog/phone-trade-in-uk-what-is-your-old-phone-worth/) explains how to compare them.

## Should I buy from Apple or from a network?

Buy wherever the total is lowest, and know what each seller is actually selling you. Apple sells the phone with no airtime attached. Its iPhone Payments terms describe a 20 month 0% APR instalment loan with Barclays Partner Finance. A network sells two things at once: a loan for the phone and an airtime plan that rises each year. Every big network now splits the two on paper.

## Why a 24 month contract is a loan, and when SIM only wins

Because the networks say so. O2's Refresh page calls the Device Plan a credit agreement. Three's FAQ says its device plans are a form of credit. EE's Flex Pay page calls it an interest free consumer credit agreement. You are borrowing the phone's price and repaying it monthly, with an airtime plan bolted on for the same term.

That is why buying the phone outright, or on Apple's own 0% plan, and adding a SIM only plan often wins on total cost. You are not committed to 24 months of airtime, and the SIM only card prints its own price rise line. Run the sum in the steps above and let the numbers decide.
