Leave a phone contract at the wrong moment and you pay for months of a service you have already left. That is on top of whatever you owe your new provider. Get the timing and the figure right, and it costs nothing beyond what you already owed. Ofcom sets the rules for what a provider can charge you to leave early. O2, EE, Vodafone and Three each publish how they apply them. This guide shows you how to get out of a phone contract early in about ten minutes. Check your date, get the figure in writing, then compare it with switching now.

## How much will it actually cost you to leave early?

Ofcom's page on being in or out of contract says an early termination charge must be based on the remaining amount of your monthly charges, minus costs the provider saves. It should not total more than the payments left on your contract.

The three networks that publish a formula agree on the shape of it, even where the number differs. O2's page on leaving says a SIM only early termination charge is your monthly tariff cost for each month left, minus any discount you get. It then takes off 4% for early receipt of payment. Its own example: three months left at £20 a month with a £5 discount comes to £45. Taking off 4% makes the final charge £43.20. EE's page on leaving says much the same shape. It takes the monthly charges for the months left, removes VAT, takes off 4%, then adds VAT back on. Its example, £30 a month with three months left, works out at £86.40. Three's page on your final bill states its early termination fee at 97% of the monthly charges left in your minimum term.

O2 also says that on a Refresh plan, ending the airtime plan does not carry an early termination charge on its own. But if you do it inside 24 months, you must pay off what is left on the device plan in full. Vodafone's page on how its fee is calculated says the amount comes from your own Charges Guide, rather than one published rate for airtime plans. Ask for yours in writing before you decide.

## Here is exactly what to do

1. Check your contract end date. Your provider's app or online account shows it, or you can ask by phone.
2. Ask your provider in writing for the exact early termination figure, not an estimate given over the phone. O2 gives an estimate through My O2 or by texting INFO to 85075; Three does the same through My3 or by texting INFO and your date of birth to 85075.
3. Compare that figure with switching now. Add the early termination charge to a new SIM only plan's cost for the months you have left on your old contract. Compare that total with just riding out your current term.
4. If switching wins, get your PAC or STAC code. Ofcom's rules mean your current provider must text it to you free of charge and reply within one minute; it stays valid for 30 days.
5. Give notice in the way your provider asks for, usually 30 days, then check your final bill against the figure you were given in writing. O2 sends its final bill 14 days after you're disconnected, Vodafone within 22 days, and Three within 38 days of your number moving to the new network.

## Can you cancel free in the first 14 days?

Yes, if you took the contract out at a distance, such as online, by phone or through an app, rather than face to face in a shop. GOV.UK's guidance on cancelling goods or services gives you 14 calendar days to change your mind, with no reason required. For a service contract, such as an airtime plan, that period runs from the day after you agreed the contract, not from when a SIM or handset arrives. Vodafone's own returns page separately gives 14 days from when your service starts. It says that cancelling a device plan inside that window also ends the linked airtime plan, as if neither had been taken out.

## Does a price rise let you leave without paying to exit?

Often, yes. Ofcom's page on price rise rights says you can exit penalty free in two cases. Your contract might not specify by how much the price could rise, or your provider might raise it by more than the contract stated. Either way, you are free to exit that contract and move to another provider without being penalised, and your provider must give at least 30 days' notice of the change. Since 17 January 2025, Ofcom has also banned inflation-linked and percentage-based price rise terms from new contracts. Any rise now has to be set out in pounds and pence, prominently, before you sign. That is what makes it possible to check whether a rise has gone beyond what you agreed in the first place.

## What happens to the price once your minimum term ends?

What happens to your price depends on what you are paying for, but your provider has to tell you before the change lands. Ofcom requires providers to send an end-of-contract notification between 10 and 40 days before your minimum term finishes, by text, email or letter. It must state the end date, what you currently pay, what changes when the term ends, and the notice needed to leave. If you stay out of contract after that, your provider must remind you at least once a year with its best available deals.

O2's help pages describe a Refresh contract splitting into an airtime plan and a device plan. Once the device plan is paid off, you only pay for airtime going forward. EE describes something similar for its Flex plans. When the airtime term ends but device payments continue, you can keep a rolling airtime plan or move to SIM only while you finish paying for the device.

## Will leaving early affect your credit file?

That depends on whether you pay the exit fee, not on the fact of leaving itself. Ofcom's guidance on being unable to pay a bill describes what happens if charges go unpaid. Your provider might restrict your account, often to emergency calls and calls to the provider only, and disconnect you if missed payments continue. Reconnecting after disconnection can mean being treated as a new customer, which brings a credit check and a deposit. Ofcom's pages we checked do not state how long an unpaid mobile charge stays on your credit file. Treat that as a question for your own credit reference agency rather than a fixed figure. Pay the stated early termination charge on the final bill and none of this applies.

## Can you keep the phone if you leave your network early?

Usually, yes. O2 and EE both treat the device you are paying off as separate from the airtime plan you are leaving. Ending the airtime side does not take the handset back. On O2 Refresh, you settle what is left on the device plan, either in full or by continuing those payments after you have left. EE describes the same split for its device agreements. Read your own provider's page before you commit, since the split is confirmed for O2 and EE and not something we found stated the same way for Vodafone or Three.

## Is your phone locked to your current network?

Not if you bought it new from 17 December 2021 onward. That is the date Ofcom's ban on selling locked handsets took effect, so any phone bought since should already accept another network's SIM. If you are keeping your number, Ofcom's switching rules mean your current provider has to text you a PAC or STAC code free of charge. It must reply within one minute, and the code stays valid for 30 days. An older phone bought before the ban may still be locked, in which case ask your current provider to remove the network lock before you switch.
