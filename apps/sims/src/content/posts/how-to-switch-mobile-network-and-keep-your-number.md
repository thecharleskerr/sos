Get the switch wrong and you can lose a mobile number you have used for years, or end up paying two providers for the same few weeks of service. Ofcom runs one process that rules out both: text to switch. It is one text message, a code only you can use, and a fixed number of days for the whole thing to complete.

Text PAC to 65075 from the phone whose number you want to keep, or STAC to 75075 if you are happy to be given a new one instead. Ofcom's own switching page states that your current provider must reply with the code within a minute, that the code stays valid for 30 days, and that your number should be ported and ready to use with your new network within one working day of you handing the code over. Every figure in this guide comes from that page and the general condition behind it, dated, so you can check it yourself before you send anything. This takes about ten minutes of your time, most of it spent waiting for two text messages.

Your number is tied to bank codes, contacts and years of accounts, so it is worth keeping unless you have a specific reason not to. The one working day porting window is not new either: Ofcom's own record of the process states it shortened porting from two working days to one back in April 2010, and that is still the figure its switching page states today.

## Here is exactly what to do

1. **Pick the deal.** Compare the card above against what you have now: the monthly price, the total contract cost, the price rise and the data, minutes and texts. That is the plan you are switching on to, so check it before you do anything else.
2. **Order the new SIM.** Buy it through the deal card, choosing a physical SIM or an eSIM if the network offers one. [Our eSIM guide](/blog/esim-uk-which-networks-offer-it/) sets out which UK networks support it.
3. **Text PAC to 65075** if you want to keep your current number, or **STAC to 75075** if you do not. Send it from the phone on the number you are switching, not from any other handset. You can also ask for the code through your current provider's online account or by phone, though texting is the route Ofcom guarantees a one minute reply on.
4. **Give the code to your new network**, either when you place your order or straight after. Do this before the 30 days run out, since an expired code means starting again.
5. **Wait for the switch.** Ofcom says it should complete within one working day of your new provider receiving the code. Your old provider cannot charge you for a notice period that runs past that date, so your old contract does not overlap the new one.

## Will I be without signal while I switch?

Only briefly, and Ofcom expects it to be short. Its switching page states that your number should normally be ported and ready to use with your new provider within one working day of your new network receiving the PAC or STAC. Keep your old SIM in until you know the new one is active. Most people see the changeover happen without a gap they notice, since the porting itself runs in the background between the two networks.

## Will I end up paying two providers at once?

No, not under Ofcom's current rules. Its switching page and General Condition C7 state that your old provider must not charge you for any notice period that runs after the switch date, a ban that has applied to mobile switching since 1 July 2019. Alongside your PAC or STAC, your current provider also has to send you the figures that affect what you owe, including any early termination charge if you are leaving mid-contract, and your remaining credit balance if you are on pay as you go. You see those numbers before you commit to the new plan, not after.

Ofcom's switching page says handing over your PAC or STAC is what your old and new providers use to coordinate the switch between themselves, specifically so that there is no double payment between the two.

## Can I switch if I am still mid-contract?

Yes. Text to switch works whether your current contract has ended or not, though leaving early can mean an early termination charge from your old provider, which it has to state in the information it sends you alongside the PAC or STAC. Weigh that charge against what moving now actually saves you. If a mid-contract price rise on your current plan is part of what is pushing you to leave, [our guide to mid-contract price rises](/blog/mid-contract-price-rises-explained/) sets out what each network currently states, in pounds and pence, so you can see whether waiting it out or switching now costs less over what is left of your term.

## PAC or STAC: which code do you need?

Use the table below to check you are texting the right word to the right number. Both figures come from Ofcom's switching page.

| Code | Text to | Keeps your number | Reply time | Valid for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PAC | 65075 | Yes | Within one minute | 30 days |
| STAC | 75075 | No | Within one minute | 30 days |

Neither code costs anything extra to request, and both come back the same way, by text. If you request either one through your online account or by phone instead, Ofcom's own guidance covers a different route into the same process, so texting the short code is the fastest way to start. If 30 days pass without you handing the code to a new network, it expires and you have to request another one.
