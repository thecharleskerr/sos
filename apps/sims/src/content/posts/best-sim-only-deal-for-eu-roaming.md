You want to land in Spain, switch on your phone and use it as you do at home, and the wrong SIM turns that week into a daily charge on top of your plan. Every UK network now sets its own EU roaming terms. Some include it on every plan. Some include it on their dearer plans only. Some charge by the day. This guide sets out which is which, from each network's own pages, with the date we checked.

Every figure below comes from our roaming table, which covers 25 networks checked between 31 August and 2 September 2026 against the network's own pages. The deal card above is re-checked every Monday. Reading this takes ten minutes.

## Which networks include EU roaming with no daily charge?

Most of the brands we list do, and what separates them is the fair use cap. O2's international page says that if your UK monthly data allowance is over 25GB, you have a roaming limit of 25GB in its Europe Zone, which covers 48 destinations. SMARTY's roaming page says you can use up to 12GB of your monthly allowance in any EU country at no extra cost. giffgaff's help centre says you get up to 5GB of data in 37 EU Zone destinations. Lebara's roaming page says roaming in Europe is included with all its SIM only plans, up to 30GB.

iD Mobile's Roam Free page states a 30GB cap, and adds that from 1 October 2024 until further notice all customers can use their full UK data allowance in 50 destinations. Tesco Mobile's Home From Home page says you can use your UK data, minutes and texts in 48 destinations at no extra cost, and states no GB cap at all. Simp's roaming page says EU and worldwide data roaming are included on every plan with no daily fee, capped at 10GB on Standard and 20GB on Premium.

Smaller brands follow the same pattern. Talkmobile and Asda Mobile each state a 5GB cap. 1pMobile states 14GB. Honest Mobile, Revolut Mobile and Klarna Mobile each state 20GB. Utility Warehouse states 8GB to 14GB by plan. Mozillion and Your Co-op Mobile state a quarter of your plan allowance. Lycamobile states caps from 12GB by bundle and spusu from 6GB. All of them are in [the full roaming table](/guides/eu-roaming-by-network).

## Which networks charge by the day, and what does a week cost?

Vodafone, EE, Sky Mobile, VOXI and Ecotalk charge by the day on the plans we checked, and Three charges by the day on its Lite plans. Vodafone's roaming guide says that on a pay monthly plan started on or after 11 August 2021, roaming in Zone B, its 52 destination Europe zone, costs £2.75 a day with a 25GB fair use policy. Vodafone's global roaming page also lists an 8 day pass at £16 and a 15 day pass at £21. EE's roaming costs page says pay monthly tariffs launched from 7 July 2021 carry a £2.59 daily charge in the EU, with a 50GB fair use cap, unless the plan includes roaming. Sky Mobile's roaming page says Roaming Passport Plus costs £2 a day across 120 destinations, with up to 25GB a billing period treated as reasonable use. VOXI's help page says European Roaming Passes start at £2.45 a day with a 20GB cap. Ecotalk's FAQs say £2 a day in Europe.

Three's Go Roam page splits by plan. Value plans include all Go Roam in Europe destinations at no extra cost, Complete plans include over 160 destinations worldwide, and Lite plans pay £2.75 a day for customers who joined or upgraded on or after 18 December 2025. Customers who joined between 1 October 2021 and 17 December 2025 pay £2 a day. The 12GB cap applies on all three. EE's page says Essentials Plus, All Rounder and Full Works plans taken since 29 August 2024 include EU roaming across 47 destinations, and Full Works adds Rest of World Zone 1.

Seven days at the daily rate is the fair comparison for a week away.

## What is a fair use cap, and how does it differ from my UK allowance?

A fair use cap is the most data you can use in the EU from your plan, and it is always the lower of the cap and your UK allowance. O2 puts it plainly: if your UK allowance is less than 25GB, that amount applies in the EU too. giffgaff's 5GB is subject to your plan having at least 5GB of UK data. Asda Mobile says up to 5GB, or your bundle cap if that is lower. VOXI says 20GB or your plan's allowance, whichever is less. So a 10GB plan on O2 gives you 10GB in Spain, not 25GB, and an unlimited plan on SMARTY gives you 12GB abroad, not unlimited.

Two things sit outside the GB figure. First, the cap runs per month or per plan, not per trip. giffgaff says its 5GB is per plan. Sky Mobile says 25GB per billing period. Second, several networks limit how long you can roam. O2's fair usage terms say using inclusive roaming for more than 63 days in any four month period is unfair use. giffgaff says inclusive roaming lasts up to 63 days per trip. Honest Mobile states a 60 day limit. Simp states 15 days a month in Europe. Tesco Mobile says roaming more than two months in any rolling four adds a surcharge of 0.35p per MB. Lebara applies a surcharge of 0.20p per MB if EU roaming exceeds UK usage over any continuous 120 day period. These matter for a long stay, not a fortnight.

What you pay over the cap is stated too. giffgaff and Asda Mobile charge 10p per MB. Sky Mobile charges £2.50 per GB. 1pMobile charges 1p a MB. If your monthly use is well under the cap you will never see these figures. If it is close, pick a network with a higher cap. [Our unlimited data guide](/blog/is-unlimited-data-worth-it/) helps you work out what you actually use.

## Is Ireland or Switzerland treated differently?

Only where a network says so, and our table records it for six brands. Vodafone's guide places the Republic of Ireland, the Isle of Man, Iceland and Norway in Zone A, which carries no daily charge on any pay monthly plan. VOXI's Zone A is the same four places and needs no pass. Three says the Republic of Ireland is exempt from its 12GB cap. SMARTY says the same, so the full plan allowance applies in Ireland. iD Mobile excludes Ireland from its fair usage policy. spusu is the only network in our table that names Switzerland, alongside Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, as part of its free roaming zone. Our table does not record Switzerland for any other network, so check the network's own destination list before you travel.

## What should I do, step by step?

1. Check your data use for the last three months in your current provider's app. Your EU data is the lower of your allowance and the network's cap, so this number tells you which caps are big enough.
2. Count the days you expect to be abroad this year. Multiply by the daily charge in the table above. Compare that with the difference in total contract cost between your plan and one that includes roaming. The card above shows the all-in monthly price, the total contract cost and the mid-contract price rise in pounds and pence, or No price rise.
3. Open the destination list on the network's own roaming page and find the countries you visit. The counts run from 37 at giffgaff to 120 at Sky Mobile, and they are not the same list.
4. Pick the plan. If it includes EU roaming with a cap above your monthly use, you are done. If you stay on a network that charges by the day, buy the pass before you travel where one costs less than the daily rate.
5. Switch and keep your number by asking your current provider for a PAC code. [Our switching guide](/blog/how-to-switch-mobile-network-and-keep-your-number/) walks through it.

## I only go abroad once a year. Does roaming still matter?

Yes, but the sum is small and you should do it rather than guess. One week a year on a network that charges by the day costs £19.25 on Vodafone or a Three Lite plan, £18.13 on EE and £14.00 on Sky Mobile, at the rates above. If a plan with roaming included costs more than that extra over the year, the daily charge is the cheaper choice for you. If it costs less, or the same, take the included plan and stop thinking about it. The card above shows the total contract cost, so this takes a minute. Do not pay for roaming you use once if the plan behind it costs more over the contract. ## What about outside the EU?

Most networks in our table charge for it, and the figures vary a lot. O2's international page says travel beyond the Europe Zone needs the O2 Travel Bolt On at £7 a day. Three's Go Roam page says its Complete plans include over 160 destinations worldwide. Sky Mobile's £2 a day covers all 120 destinations in Roaming Passport Plus, EU and beyond, as one zone. EE's roaming costs page says Full Works plans include Rest of World Zone 1 as well as the EU. Tesco Mobile says data in rest of the world destinations can cost up to £5 per MB. Ecotalk says data cannot be used outside the EU at all.

Simp is the exception. Its roaming page says worldwide data roaming is included on every plan with no daily fee: a 5G speed allowance of 3GB on Standard or 5GB on Premium, then unlimited data at 4G speed across more than 130 countries. Klarna Mobile states 20GB of global roaming across 162 destinations in five regions. Revolut Mobile's 20GB covers 30 EU countries and the US. If you travel beyond Europe, that is the column to check, not the EU one.

## Does 5G work abroad?

Only where a page says so, and most do not promise it. giffgaff's help centre says you can use 5G in the UK, but access to 5G when roaming depends on several factors. Simp states a 5G speed allowance on its worldwide roaming, 3GB on Standard and 5GB on Premium, then 4G. No other page we checked promises 5G in the EU, so treat 5G abroad as a bonus rather than a reason to choose a plan. If you are unsure whether you need 5G at all, [our 5G guide](/blog/do-i-need-5g/) covers it.
