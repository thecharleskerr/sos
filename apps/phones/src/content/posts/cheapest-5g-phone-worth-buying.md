The cheapest 5G phone worth buying is not the one with the lowest price tag. It is the one whose maker still promises updates for it on the day your SIM only plan ends. Two years of payments for a phone that stops receiving security updates after one year is money spent for nothing.

On 2 September 2026 we checked the UK price and the stated update commitment for the entry 5G model in three ranges people search for. Those are Apple's iPhone, Samsung's Galaxy A series and Google's Pixel a-series. Every figure below comes from the maker's own UK page, dated. Where a maker states nothing, we say so rather than guess. A wrong number printed here costs you more than it costs us.

## What is the cheapest 5G phone worth buying right now?

Samsung's Galaxy A17 5G, from £199 for the base 128GB model, was the lowest verified price of the three we checked. Samsung states that on its own UK buy page, checked 2 September 2026. Apple's iPhone 17e came next, from £599 for its base 256GB model, per Apple's UK newsroom announcement of the phone. That announcement also states the 17e replaced the iPhone 16e at the same starting price, with twice the entry storage. Google's Store UK page for the Pixel 10a stated no UK price on the same date, so we print none here either.

Price alone does not make a phone worth buying. The three makers state very different update commitments. That number decides how many years the phone stays safe to use, not the chip inside it. Samsung states six years of OS upgrades and six years of security updates. Google states seven years of OS and security updates. No Apple page we checked states a fixed number of years for the iPhone at all. That field is marked not stated rather than assumed to match the others.

## Will a £199 phone's camera let you down?

Not for most everyday photos, on the hardware each maker states. Samsung's UK page for the Galaxy A17 5G states a triple rear camera. That is a 50 megapixel main lens with optical image stabilisation, a 5 megapixel ultra-wide lens and a 2 megapixel macro lens. Apple's page for the iPhone 17e states a single 48 megapixel Fusion camera. Google's page for the Pixel 10a states a 48 megapixel dual rear camera. None of the three pages we checked ranks one against another. If the camera is what decides it for you, look at sample photos on the maker's own page rather than take a number alone as the answer.

## Will the battery survive a full day?

On the makers' own figures, all three should. Apple's iPhone 17e specification page states up to 26 hours of video playback. Samsung's Galaxy A17 5G page states a 5,000mAh battery with 25W fast charging. Google's Pixel 10a page describes an all-day battery lasting over 30 hours. These figures are not measured the same way. Apple's is a video playback test, Samsung's is raw battery capacity, and Google's is a stated hours figure. Treat them as a guide to which phone is built for longer use, not as a direct comparison of identical tests.
