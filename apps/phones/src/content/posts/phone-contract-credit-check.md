A failed credit check on a phone contract does not have to mean no new phone. It means the network will not lend you that particular one, because a pay monthly contract with a phone attached is a loan as well as an airtime plan. Get that distinction wrong and you either give up on a phone you could still afford outright, or reapply for a loan you never needed. This guide sets out, from the networks' own pages, why the credit check happens, which plans skip it, and exactly what to do next if you are turned down. It takes about ten minutes.

## Why does a phone contract need a credit check?

Because the network is lending you the phone. O2 states plainly that a device plan is a loan agreement that lets you spread the cost of a product into lower monthly payments, and that because it is a loan agreement, O2 needs to run a credit check, subject to status. EE describes Flex Pay the same way: credit for Flex Pay is subject to a credit check, your status and assessment, on top of the ordinary 24 month EE airtime plan it also requires. Three calls its own device plans a form of credit, interest free, spreading the cost of a phone over 12, 24 or 36 months. Each of those is a separate credit agreement for the handset, sitting on top of the airtime plan for your calls, texts and data. The handset loan is the part that gets checked.

## Why doesn't a SIM only plan usually need one?

Because there is nothing to lend. A rolling SIM only plan is airtime only, paid for as you go, with no phone attached and so no loan to assess. giffgaff's help pages state this directly: giffgaff will not do a credit check before you join on a SIM only contract, and a credit check only applies if you choose to spread the cost of a device using giffgaff's own loan option. SMARTY says much the same about its own plans. Its page, titled No Credit Check SIM only Deals, states that SMARTY SIM only deals, paid upfront on a rolling monthly plan, do not require a credit check. Lycamobile's student plans go further still. Verified through Student Beans, Lycamobile states they carry no contract and no credit check at all.

| Plan | What it is | Credit check | In the provider's own words |
|---|---|---|---|
| O2 Device Plan | A phone on finance | Yes | "O2 will need to run a credit check, and it's subject to status" |
| EE Flex Pay | A phone on finance | Yes | "Credit for Flex Pay is subject to a credit check, your status and assessment" |
| giffgaff SIM only | Rolling SIM only, no phone | No | "giffgaff won't do a credit check before you join on a SIM only contract" |
| SMARTY SIM only | Rolling SIM only, no phone | No | "SMARTY SIM-only deals ... do not require a credit check" |

Not every network publishes a clear statement either way, so this table only names the ones whose own pages say so. Where a provider states no policy, treat a credit check as likely and ask before you apply.

## Will a credit check affect my credit rating?

Not the eligibility step itself. O2's own eligibility checker page states this isn't a credit check, so your credit rating won't be affected, and that O2 carries out a full credit assessment later in the checkout journey, once you actually apply. That earlier step is meant to tell you whether you are likely to pass before you commit to applying for the device plan. The check that can show on your file is the one run when you submit that application. We found no O2, EE or Three page stating how long that search stays on file, so that detail is not stated here rather than guessed at, and no page names which credit reference agency any of them use.

## Here is exactly what to do if you are turned down

1. Check which part was actually refused. O2 and EE both split a pay monthly bill into an airtime plan and a separate device credit agreement, so a refusal may only affect the loan on the phone, not the airtime.
2. Buy the phone outright, or Apple or Samsung Certified Refurbished, instead of financing it. Apple's UK refurbished page states a new battery, a new outer shell and a one year warranty. Samsung's Certified Re-Newed page states a new battery and the same two year warranty as a new phone. Neither route is a loan, so there is nothing to check.
3. Pair the phone with a rolling SIM only plan that skips the credit check, such as giffgaff or SMARTY above, or [compare SIM only deals](https://saveonsims.co.uk/) and the [no-contract picks](https://saveonsims.co.uk/deals/no-contract/) on Save on Sims.
4. Consider a cheaper handset. A less expensive phone needs a smaller loan, or a smaller amount to save before buying it outright. For scale, Samsung lists the Galaxy A17 5G at £199 on its UK site. Browse the [phones this site tracks](/phones/) for others.
5. If you would rather stay pay monthly, ask whether a shorter device plan term is on offer. Three's device plans run 12, 24 or 36 months and EE's Flex Pay runs 12, 24, 36 or 48 months, so a shorter term is not unusual, though neither states that a shorter term changes whether an application is approved.
6. Add up the total cost either way before you decide. Our guide on [how much you should pay for a phone contract](/blog/how-much-should-you-pay-for-a-phone-contract/) walks through that sum.

## Can I still get a good phone if I am refused credit?

Yes. Buying outright, new or refurbished, does not lower your rights. gov.uk states that you have the same rights when you are supplied with second hand goods as with new goods, covering satisfactory quality and being fit for purpose, from any business that sells directly to you. On top of that, Apple's own refurbished devices come with a new battery, a new outer shell and a one year warranty, and Samsung's Certified Re-Newed devices carry genuine Samsung parts and a two year warranty. A phone bought outright from either maker's own store is not a lesser phone. It is the same handset, without the loan attached to it. See our guide on [buying a refurbished iPhone](/blog/should-you-buy-a-refurbished-iphone/) for what to check before you order one.

There is also a cost argument for splitting the phone from the plan, refusal or not. giffgaff and SMARTY, the two providers above whose pages rule out a credit check on SIM only, also rule out a mid-contract price rise. giffgaff states there won't be any mid-contract price rises on an 18 month contract, and SMARTY states prices stay the same from when you first sign up. A rolling SIM only plan bought alongside a phone you already own, or bought outright, is often the cheaper way to get a working number, not just the fallback if you fail a check.

## Does an unpaid phone bill make it harder to pass a credit check later?

It can, if the account is disconnected. Ofcom's guidance on disputing a bill states that if you do not pay, your provider might restrict your account, typically to emergency calls and calls to the provider only, then disconnect you if payments keep being missed. It also states that disconnected customers might be treated as new customers and have to go through a credit check and pay a deposit to reconnect. That is a reason to sort out an existing balance, or move to a SIM only plan you can clearly afford, rather than let a contract lapse into arrears while you wait to apply again.

A failed credit check on the phone is not a failed credit check on the whole idea of a new number. Buy the handset outright or refurbished, take a rolling SIM only plan that skips the credit check, and you often end up paying less than the pay monthly contract you were refused.
