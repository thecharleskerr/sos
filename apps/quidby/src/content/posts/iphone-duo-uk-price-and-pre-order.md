## What is the iPhone Duo?

Apple's own newsroom calls it the first foldable iPhone, made from grade 5 titanium polished to a mirror finish, checked 9 September 2026. Opened, it has a 7.6 inch inner display. Closed, the 5.4 inch outer display gives 90 per cent of the screen area of the iPhone 18 Pro, both figures from Apple's newsroom, checked the same date. It comes in two colours, star white and night sky. Inside sits the new A20 Pro chip, the same chip Apple put in the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max. Apple pairs it here with what it describes as a dual battery architecture and a custom vapour chamber for cooling, checked 9 September 2026. ## Why does a first generation folding phone change the contract sum?

A high outright price pushes up every other figure that follows from it. £1,999 for the 256GB iPhone Duo, checked 9 September 2026, is £700 more than the £1,299 Apple charges for the 256GB iPhone 18 Pro, checked the same date. Both phones use the same A20 Pro chip. A network spreading that extra £700 over a contract has to charge more each month just to cover the handset, before airtime is added. The mid-contract price rise you may have read about does not sit on that device cost. O2's own pricing page states that its airtime plans go up by £2.50 a month each April, with the cost of the device remaining frozen, checked 2 September 2026. EE's own terms state a similar pattern, a rise of £2.50 a month each 31 March on its plans, checked 4 September 2026. EE is never sold as an affiliate deal on this site, so it appears here only for its published policy, not as a deal. Not every network raises prices at all: SMARTY and giffgaff both state no price rise on the plans they sell now, checked 2 September 2026. Judge the iPhone Duo by the same test as any other phone. Add the monthly price to the contract length, plus any upfront cost and the network's stated price rise, then compare that total to £1,999 plus a SIM only plan for the same months. Because the outright price starts so high, expect that outright plus SIM only route to usually come out cheaper, unless a network subsidises the handset heavily enough to close the gap.
