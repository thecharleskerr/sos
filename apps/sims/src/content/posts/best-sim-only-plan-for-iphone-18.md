Buy the iPhone 18 outright and then put it on the wrong SIM only plan, and a mid-contract price rise or a roaming charge hands back part of the money you saved. The best SIM only plan for an iPhone 18 is one on a network that offers eSIM, states no mid-contract price rise on its own pages, and includes the EU roaming you need. Twelve UK brands meet the first two tests on their own pages, checked between 2 and 4 September 2026, and the table below lists them with their roaming line. The cards above are this week's picks, re-checked every Monday, so compare their total cost first and then read the roaming line.

## Is the iPhone 18 eSIM only, and does it matter?

Apple's UK page for the iPhone 18 Pro describes it as 5G and eSIM but does not state whether the UK model keeps a physical SIM tray, checked 9 September 2026, so that is not stated here. The iPhone Duo, announced the same day, is eSIM only: Apple's specifications page states it has no physical SIM tray, checked 9 September 2026. Pre-orders for the Pro and Pro Max open at 1:00 pm on Saturday 12 September 2026, with the phones on sale from 18 September; the Duo follows on 16 October. Our sister site has the [prices and dates](https://saveonsmartphones.co.uk/blog/iphone-18-pro-uk-price-and-pre-order/) and the [iPhone 18 Pro page](https://saveonsmartphones.co.uk/phones/iphone-18-pro/) with this week's contract picks.

It matters less than it sounds, because every network in the table below states eSIM on its own pages. What differs is how you get it. SMARTY lets you choose eSIM at checkout or swap in the dashboard. giffgaff activates it through its app only. iD Mobile sends a QR code by email and supports phones only, not tablets or wearables. Lebara asks existing customers to contact support. Talkmobile, Lycamobile, 1pMobile and Ecotalk send a QR code by email, Mozillion within one working day, and Honest Mobile says delivery is instant. Simp is eSIM only, with every plan activated by a QR code in its app. Asda Mobile lets you pick eSIM at checkout. O2 offers it on Big Bundle and rolling SIM only plans, Vodafone on all pay monthly and pay as you go SIM only plans, and Three on 30 day, 12 month and 24 month SIM only plans at no price difference, all from their own pages, checked 2 September 2026. Our guide to [which networks offer eSIM](/blog/esim-uk-which-networks-offer-it/) has the detail for every network we track.

## Which networks say there is no price rise?

The point of buying the phone outright is that the whole cost is fixed on day one, and a SIM only plan with a stated mid-contract rise undoes that. Twelve brands state no rise on their own pages. SMARTY says its prices stay the same from when you sign up, checked 2 September 2026. giffgaff says there will not be any mid-contract price rises on its 18 month contracts, and iD Mobile says a fixed rise applies only to its phone plans, not SIM only, both checked 2 September 2026. Lebara says no annual or mid-contract rises and that it has not raised prices since 2020. Simp says there is no contract and no mid-contract price rise. Talkmobile's own page says all its plans come with no annual price rises. Lycamobile has a page titled no mid-contract price rises. Asda Mobile states none on its 12 and 24 month SIM only plans. 1pMobile, Mozillion, Honest Mobile and Ecotalk each state no rise, the last four checked 4 September 2026.

The host networks are different. O2 and Vodafone state £2.50 a month each April on their airtime and SIM only plans, and Three states £1.80, £1.90 or £2.30 a month each 1 April by plan size for customers joining from 9 November 2025, all checked 2 September 2026. That does not rule them out; it means their total over the term is the monthly price plus the rise for every April inside it. The [price rise calculator](/price-rise-calculator/) works it out for your own figures.

## Here is exactly what to do

1. Check which host network has signal where you live and work. Only O2, Vodafone, Three and EE own masts; every brand above rents one of them, and the [networks page](/networks/) shows who runs on whom.
2. Open the cards above and compare the total cost over the term, not the monthly price. A card on a no-rise network has nothing to add; on O2, Vodafone or Three add the April rise for every April inside the term.
3. Read the roaming line. If you travel in the EU, take a plan whose cap covers a trip; giffgaff's page, for instance, limits inclusive roaming to 63 days a trip, and O2 states 25GB of Europe Zone data on its plans.
4. Check how the network delivers its eSIM, from the table above, so it is ready when the phone arrives on 18 September.
5. Keep your number by texting PAC to 65075 before you switch; the code is valid for 30 days. Our [switching guide](/blog/how-to-switch-mobile-network-and-keep-your-number/) has the rest.
6. [See this week's no price rise deals](/deals/no-price-rise/) and pick the one whose total cost and roaming line fit, then order the eSIM.

## Should I take a 30 day rolling plan or a 12 month one?

A rolling plan suits a new phone bought outright, because nothing ties you to the plan and you can move the moment a better card appears on a Monday. Several of the no-rise brands above sell only rolling plans: SMARTY, Simp, 1pMobile and Ecotalk. A 12 month plan can be cheaper per month on some networks, and on a no-rise network the price you sign at is the price you pay throughout, so the choice is between flexibility and a lower fixed rate rather than between safe and risky. Our guide on [pay as you go against SIM only](/blog/pay-as-you-go-vs-sim-only/) sets out the three shapes of plan, and the [short contract pick above](/deals/no-contract/) shows this week's best rolling deal.

## Do I still need to worry about roaming on a no-rise plan?

Yes, because roaming and price rises are separate lines on the card. A network can promise no rise and still charge for the EU, as Ecotalk's page does at £2 a day, or include it with a cap, as SMARTY's 12GB and Lebara's 30GB fair use limits do, checked 31 August and 1 September 2026. O2 includes up to 25GB of Europe Zone data but says inclusive roaming is for periodic travel, with surcharges after 63 days in any four months. Our [EU roaming guide](/guides/eu-roaming-by-network) carries every network's line on one page, and the [EU roaming pick](/blog/best-sim-only-deal-for-eu-roaming/) shows this week's best card for a trip.

## Is a SIM only plan really cheaper than a contract for the iPhone 18?

Usually, and the sum settles it. Apple's UK shop lists the 256GB iPhone 18 Pro at £1,299 or £43.30 a month for 30 months at 0% APR, checked 9 September 2026. Add a SIM only plan for the same months and you have a total that does not move on a no-rise network. A contract beats it only when its monthly price times its length, plus upfront cost and the network's stated rise, comes in lower.
