The cheapest iPhone 18 deal is the one that costs you the least by the day the contract ends. Choosing by the monthly figure alone is how people pay more for the same phone. On 2 September 2026 Apple had not announced the iPhone 18 or published a UK price, so there is no deal to name yet. This guide gives you the method instead. Every figure in it comes from the seller's or the regulator's own pages and carries its checked date, so on launch day you can run the sums yourself.

## Is the iPhone 18 on sale in the UK yet?

No. We checked apple.com/uk on 2 September 2026. Apple's UK buy page listed the iPhone 17, the iPhone 17 Pro, the iPhone Air, the iPhone 17e and the iPhone 16, and no iPhone 18. The iPhone 17 was listed from £799, or £26.63 a month over 30 months at 0% APR. There was no iPhone 18 page and no UK price. Apple's newsroom carried no iPhone 18 announcement on the same date.

That means any iPhone 18 price you see before Apple publishes one is a guess. We do not print guesses. ## What makes one iPhone 18 deal cheaper than another?

The total you pay over the term, not the monthly figure. The sum is simple: monthly price times the number of months, plus the upfront cost, plus every stated price rise for the months it applies.

A low monthly price with a large upfront payment can cost more than a higher monthly price with nothing upfront. A 36 month device plan lowers the monthly figure and adds a year of payments. The card on this site prints the total so you never do the multiplication in your head.

The price rise matters because it is now a fixed number. Ofcom's page on the ban says that from 17 January 2025 providers are prohibited from including inflation-linked or percentage-based price rise terms in new contracts, and that any rise must be set out in pounds and pence at the point of sale. If a seller cannot tell you the rise in pounds, walk away.

## What should I do on launch day?

Run these six steps in order and let the totals decide.

1. Open the [homepage](/) on or after launch day and note the total cost on each iPhone 18 card. Ignore the monthly price for now. The total is the number you compare.
2. Read the price rise line on the same card. It says the rise in pounds and pence, or No price rise. The card's total does not include the rise, so count the months after it lands, multiply, and add that to the total. Do it for every rise date inside the term.
3. Price the phone on its own. Apple's UK buy page lists the SIM free price and the 0% APR monthly option for each storage size. Note the size the network deal actually gives you, because the two must match.
4. Add a SIM only plan to Apple's price for the same number of months. Pick one on our sister site, [Save on Sims](https://saveonsims.co.uk/), where every card prints the same total and price rise line. Phone plus SIM only is the number the network deal has to beat.
5. Subtract trade-in on both sides. Get an estimate for your old phone from Apple's Trade In page and from the network. Use the same old phone value in both sums so the comparison is fair.
6. Take the lower total, then check the data, minutes, texts and roaming on the card cover what you use. A cheap total with an allowance you will exceed is not cheap. If data is the allowance you worry about, Save on Sims' guide to [the best UK network for data](https://saveonsims.co.uk/blog/best-uk-network-for-data/) helps you pick.

## Should I wait for the iPhone 18 price to drop?

We found no official page that schedules iPhone price cuts, so we cannot tell you when or whether the iPhone 18 will get cheaper. What Apple's own pages show is the alternative to waiting. On 2 September 2026 apple.com/uk still listed the iPhone 16 alongside the iPhone 17, so the older model was still on sale next to the newer one. Whether the iPhone 17 stays listed once the iPhone 18 arrives is for Apple's buy page to show on the day. Apple's refurbished store lists iPhones at up to 15% off, with a one year warranty, a new battery and a new outer shell.

Waiting does not remove the price rise either. Three's terms for customers joining on or after 9 November 2025 put it on 1 April. EE's put it on 31 March during the minimum term. O2 says each April and Vodafone says 1 April. Whatever month you sign, read the rise line on the card and count every rise date that falls inside your term.

## Is trade-in worth it?

Yes, when you treat it as a cut to the upfront cost and put it into the total on both sides. Apple's iPhone 17 page offered £30 to £680 off when you trade in an iPhone X or newer, checked 2 September 2026, so the gap between a worn old phone and a recent one is wide. Apple's Trade In page says the credit lowers the amount you finance if you pay monthly. It says the old phone must be sent within 14 days of receiving the new one. And it says that if the value changes after inspection, Apple contacts you to ask how you want to proceed.

Networks run their own trade-in schemes, and [our trade-in guide](/blog/phone-trade-in-uk-what-is-your-old-phone-worth/) explains how each one pays out. Their values are not on pages we can quote, so get a written quote before you rely on one.

## Should I buy from Apple or from a network?

Buy wherever the total is lowest, and know what each seller is actually selling you. Apple sells the phone with no airtime attached. On 2 September 2026 its buy page listed the iPhone 17 at £26.63 a month over 30 months at 0% APR. Its financing page names Creation and PayPal Credit as the finance providers, and its iPhone Payments terms describe a separate 20 month 0% APR instalment loan with Barclays Partner Finance. Either way it is credit for the phone alone. A network sells two things at once: a loan for the phone and an airtime plan that rises each year. All four networks in the table split the two on paper.

| Apple | 0% APR monthly payments, 30 months on the buy page, 20 months in the iPhone Payments terms | None, you choose your own SIM | Not applicable | Not applicable |
| O2 | Refresh Device Plan, 3 to 36 months at 0% APR | £2.50 a month on voice plans | Each April | No, the device cost is frozen |

| EE | Flex Pay, 12, 24, 36 or 48 months, interest free | £1.50 a month | 31 March in the minimum term | Not stated |

All figures are from each seller's own pages, checked 2 September 2026. Three's tiers apply to customers joining on or after 9 November 2025: £1.80 on plans of 4GB or less, £1.90 from 5GB to 99GB, £2.30 at 100GB or over. EE's £1.50 applies to new and re-contracting mobile customers from 31 March 2025. EE is listed here for its published policy only.

Ofcom has banned networks from selling locked handsets since 17 December 2021, so a network phone is not tied to that network, but your airtime minimum term still is. Read the early termination terms before you sign.

## Is a 24 month contract a loan, and when does SIM only win?

Yes, the phone part is a loan, because the networks say so. O2's Refresh page calls the Device Plan a credit agreement. Three's FAQ says its device plans are a form of credit. EE's Flex Pay page calls it an interest free consumer credit agreement. You are borrowing the phone's price and repaying it monthly, with an airtime plan bolted on for the same term.

That is why buying the phone outright, or on Apple's own 0% APR plan, and adding a SIM only plan can win on total cost, which [our contract or outright guide](/blog/iphone-18-contract-vs-buying-outright/) works through step by step. You are not committed to 24 months of airtime, and the SIM only card prints its own price rise line. [Save on Sims](https://saveonsims.co.uk/) has the SIM only cards for that half of the sum, and our [guides](/blog/) will cover this phone in more depth once its price and the deals arrive.

If you are a student, check the discounts before you compare. O2's student offer page says 20% off the Airtime Plan on a Refresh phone contract through UNiDAYS, and Three's says 20% off the airtime with the code STUDY20, with device repayments excluded, both checked 1 September 2026. Both discounts apply to the airtime and not to the phone loan, so put them into the airtime line of your sum.
