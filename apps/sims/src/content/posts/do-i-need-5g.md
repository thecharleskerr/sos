Pay more for a "5G plan" and you can end up buying a badge you already had for free, on a phone that cannot use it, in a postcode where it barely reaches. That is three separate ways to waste money on one word.

5G is not a price tier. On every network we checked, it is included on SIM only plans at no extra cost. What decides whether you actually get it is your phone, your postcode and nothing else. Sort those two things out first and the plan choice gets much simpler.

## What 5G actually needs

Three things have to line up together: a phone with a 5G modem, a plan that includes 5G, and coverage where you are. Miss any one of the three and you are on 4G regardless of what the SIM packet says.

Ofcom's own guidance on mobile reception says some older phones only support 3G or 4G connections, and some people have a data plan that is not 5G enabled. Coverage itself is not uniform either. Ofcom's most recent reporting on the 5G rollout puts outdoor 5G coverage from at least one operator at 97% of the UK, but that is a national figure, not a promise for your street. The only way to know for certain is to check your own postcode.

Ofcom runs a free tool for exactly this, called Map Your Mobile, at ofcom.org.uk. Put in your postcode. It shows, network by network, whether 4G and 5G signal is likely outdoors and indoors, built from a mix of operator data and real user reports. It takes about a minute.

## Which SIM only networks include 5G at no extra cost

Every SIM only network we checked, O2, Vodafone, Three, SMARTY, giffgaff, iD Mobile and Lebara, includes 5G at no extra cost, and EE states the same for its own plans. We checked each network's own pages directly, not a reseller or a forum, and here is what each one currently states.

| Network | 5G on SIM only | What their page says |
|---|---|---|
| O2 | Included, no extra cost | All Pay Monthly and Pay As You Go plans include 5G |
| Vodafone | Included, no extra cost | Standard across SIM only and Pay monthly, except the 4G only Vodafone Basics range |
| Three | Included, no extra cost | All Three SIMs are described as 5G ready |
| EE | Included, no extra cost | Described here for information only. EE is not sold as a deal on this site |
| SMARTY | Included, no extra cost | Advertised on its own site as "5G at no extra cost" |
| giffgaff | Included, no extra cost | Available on all plans, though some older SIMs need 5G switching on manually |
| iD Mobile | Included, no extra cost | Included on all SIM only contracts, running on the Three network |
| Lebara | Included, no extra cost | Included across its plan range, needs a 5G compatible handset |

Every one of those states no extra charge for 5G on the plan itself. None of them state a speed figure we could stand behind, so we have left speed out of this table entirely.

If your current network is not on that list, or a deal you are looking at does not mention 5G either way, treat it as not stated rather than assuming.

## Here is exactly what to do

1. Check your phone's exact model against its maker's own specification page for a 5G modem. Do not guess from the age of the phone alone.
2. Look up whether the plan you have, or the one you are considering, includes 5G at no extra cost. Use the table above as a starting point and check the network's own page for anything not listed here.
3. Go to ofcom.org.uk and run your postcode through Map Your Mobile to see 4G and 5G coverage for each network at your address, and anywhere else you spend most of your day.
4. If 5G coverage where you live is thin or absent, do not pay a premium chasing it. Pick your plan on data, contract length and the mid contract price rise instead, all of which are on the card above.
5. If you do have a 5G phone and solid coverage, it is included at no extra cost on every network in the table above. There is no separate box to tick or extra fee to accept.
6. Re-run the coverage check if you move house or change your main daily location. Coverage is measured by postcode, not by network name.

## Will 5G drain my phone's battery faster?

Not stated, as far as any official source we checked says. Three's own page on retiring its 3G network states that 4G and 5G networks are both more energy efficient than the older 3G network. It adds that devices on 4G or 5G tend to have longer battery life than the same device on 3G. No network we checked publishes a direct comparison of 5G against 4G battery use, so we are not going to invent one. If your phone's battery life worries you, that is a question for the phone, covered in our guide on [whether unlimited data is worth it](/blog/is-unlimited-data-worth-it/), not a reason to avoid 5G itself.

## Is 4G about to be switched off?

No, not based on anything Ofcom or the networks state. What has actually closed is 3G. Ofcom confirms EE, Vodafone, Three and O2 have all completed their 3G switch-off. The next stage is 2G, which Ofcom says operators have committed to closing by 2033 at the latest, and EE states it will start closing from 2029. 4G is not mentioned in any of that as being withdrawn. If your phone only reaches 4G, it keeps working. It simply will not reach 5G, which is a coverage and hardware question, not an expiry date.

## Will my current phone even work on 5G?

Only if it has a 5G modem built in. Ofcom's guidance states that roughly half of mobile handsets in use are 5G capable, which means the other half are not, regardless of plan or coverage. Age is a rough guide only, not proof either way: check your specific model against the manufacturer's own specification page rather than assuming from the release year. If your phone genuinely cannot do 5G, none of this changes what to look for in a SIM only plan. ## What to check before you switch

Check two things before you switch: whether your phone can use 5G, and whether Ofcom's coverage checker shows it reaching your address. So, do you need 5G itself? Only if your phone can use it and your postcode gets it, and neither one costs anything extra to check. None of the SIM only networks we checked charge more for 5G itself, so paying a premium chasing the letter is pointless. Everything else, price, data, the length of the contract and what happens to the price partway through, matters more than the letter in front of the G.
